Research interests

Investigating flow across scales

Our research numerically investigates the physics of fluid flow at micro-, meso- and macro-scales, spanning multiphysics phenomena coupled to fluid physics — particle suspensions, applied electric and magnetic fields, chemical reactions and combustible flow, and radiative or neutron transport.

Lattice Boltzmann method development

The group's primary tool is the lattice Boltzmann method — a kinetic-theory scheme derived from a discrete Boltzmann equation. We develop finite-volume and total-variation-diminishing formulations on unstructured meshes, and multiple-relaxation-time, cascaded and central-moment collision models, together with multigrid and preconditioning strategies for fast steady-state convergence.

Compressible and high-speed flows

Finite-difference lattice Boltzmann methods combined with high-order Runge–Kutta time stepping and WENO reconstruction for supersonic and high-Mach flows over wedges and forward-facing steps. This direction is being carried forward with ISRO-RESPOND toward complex geometries, high-Knudsen-number flows, shock–vortex interactions and re-entry problems.

Multiphase flow and interface dynamics

Phase-field lattice Boltzmann approaches for droplets, bubbles, pinch-off and interfacial flows steered by surface tension. A convergent multigrid algorithm within the LB framework accelerates steady multiphase solutions, with extensions toward transient and phase-change problems such as pool boiling.

Thermal convection

Rayleigh–Bénard and mixed convection with anisotropic thermal diffusion in tilted and complex two-dimensional enclosures, including entropy-generation analysis and axisymmetric thermal-LB formulations.

Non-Newtonian and complex fluids

Power-law fluid flows over obstacles, in driven cavities and corrugated channels using MRT-LBM, with assessment of collision models for shear-thinning and shear-thickening behaviour.

Fluid–particle and granular systems

Coupled discrete-element and lattice Boltzmann (DEM–LBM) methods for particle suspensions, granular impact and avalanches, electro-rheology of suspensions, and experimental studies of air-jet impingement on granular beds.

Methodology

The lattice Boltzmann method sits at the centre of our work, but we also develop and apply other numerical and particle-based methods — finite-volume schemes, discrete-element methods, phase-field models and smoothed-particle hydrodynamics — and combine them where multiphysics coupling demands it. Simulations run on high-performance-computing infrastructure, including GPU-accelerated solvers.
